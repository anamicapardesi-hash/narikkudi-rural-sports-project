# Narikkudi Rural Sports Project

**Community-led sports access in Narikkudi Block, Virudhunagar District, Tamil Nadu · SBI Youth for India Fellowship, 2024–25**

I developed and implemented this field project with DHAN Foundation to make regular sport more accessible to children where structured coaching, suitable play space and sporting pathways were limited. The work combined direct sessions with resource activation, school and parent engagement, local volunteers and advocacy with public institutions.

## At a glance

| Area | Project evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Reach | **80+ children** engaged in structured sports activities across the pilot |
| Participation | Later project records identify **25 regular** and **30+ occasional** participants; these are subsets or differently defined cohorts and should not be added to total reach |
| Resources | A **Sports Resource Centre** was established with SBI Sahyog support, placing equipment under local custody |
| Space | A temporary practice space at the Union Office parking area was used while advocacy for a permanent playground continued |
| Inclusion | Girls' interest was documented through school-based activities; social norms, timing and mobility remained barriers |
| Continuity | Local volunteers contributed, but employment and other commitments made sessions irregular after the fellow-led phase |

These figures come from project monitoring and retrospective synthesis. They are **not independently verified causal impact estimates**.

## The challenge

The field assessment found irregular access to structured sport, limited safe play space and underused sports equipment. In the relevant government-school context, Physical Education Teacher posts had reportedly been absent for more than 12 years. Girls faced additional barriers to participation, and children had weak links to zonal or district competition opportunities.

## What I did

- Conducted baseline conversations and surveys with children, parents and local stakeholders.
- Ran structured practice sessions, introduced football and supported community and school-based sports activities, including sessions with girls.
- Mobilised local volunteers and coaches, organised a sports camp and school sports day, and engaged families to build acceptance.
- Established a Sports Resource Centre through SBI Sahyog and worked with schools and teachers on equipment custody.
- Used an interim public practice space and engaged the Panchayat, Block Development Office and sports authorities on access to a lasting playground and sporting pathways.

## Assessment approach

The [retrospective impact assessment](#evidence-and-limitations) applies a **Theory of Change**, **Logical Framework Analysis**, baseline-to-observed-outcome comparison, stakeholder analysis, inclusion review and sustainability assessment. It separates delivered activities and outputs from observed short-term changes and longer-term intended impacts.

| Level | Example | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Sessions, events, Sports Resource Centre, temporary practice space | Documented in project records |
| Early outcome | Repeat participation, greater visibility of sport, qualitative parent support | Observed or reported; measurement limits apply |
| Intended impact | Lasting physical and life-skill gains, sustained inclusion, formal sporting progression | Requires endline and longer follow-up |

## What the pilot taught me

Equipment and enthusiasm alone did not guarantee a reliable programme. Volunteer retention was the largest continuity gap: jobs, education and other commitments interrupted sessions. My post-pilot research is examining a shared delivery model linking schools, nearby physical-education institutions or BPEd placements, sports authorities, local government and community volunteers. **This partnership model is a proposal under development, not an implemented result.**

## Evidence and limitations

This case study draws on fellowship field documentation, baseline interactions, attendance and registration records, Sports Resource Centre and event records, and stakeholder engagement. There was no independent endline, comparison group or validated pre/post fitness or psychosocial assessment. Attendance was not consistently disaggregated across all relevant groups. Long-term health, education, employment and sporting career effects cannot yet be claimed.

A next evaluation should track unique participants and repeat attendance, girls' access and retention, equipment use, volunteer continuity, sessions delivered without fellow support, competition referrals, and outcomes at 3, 6 and 12 months. Beneficiary and parent feedback should be included.
